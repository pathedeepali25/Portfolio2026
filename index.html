01·B Smart Glasses by Lenskart NDA · Overview only

Shaping a new smart wearable, end-to-end.

Led 0→1 design for Lenskart's first smart-eyewear category, from user research through to product-ready output. The brief: turn unfamiliar hardware into a calm, ten-second first impression.

ClientLenskart
RoleProduct Designer, end-to-end
Type0 → 1 · Smart wearable
Year2024 – 2025
01The problem

Hardware that has to teach itself.

People had never used a category like this before. The hardware was rich, the gestures were new, and the value had to land in the first 90 seconds — or the box quietly closed.

The work was less about features and more about translation: turning capability into something you'd want to keep on your face.

02Approach

Research first. Then everything else.

I led primary research with people who had never used the form factor, mapping where they hesitated, what they expected, and what they reached for instead. That shaped the experience principles for the launch.

  • Calm first run, pairing under 90 seconds with zero jargon
  • Gestures with grace, three core motions, each with a clear undo
  • Quiet by default, notifications surface only when context warrants
  • One mental model shared between hardware, companion app and box
03Surfaces

From pairing to everyday use.

The brief was hardware. The work was about permission — getting people to trust something new on their face.
04Outcome

Shipped, aligned, quietly confident.

The full launch is under NDA, but the company-side wins are easy to name:

  • A shared visual + interaction language across hardware, app, and unboxing
  • Decisions teams could defend without me in the room
  • Research grounding that survived contact with engineering reality

03·AjnaLens Design System

Design once. Bring consistency everywhere.

As the product scaled, inconsistencies started creating friction in both design and development. I built a structured system to bring clarity, consistency and efficiency to the whole experience.

ProjectAjnaLens Design System
RoleProduct Designer
TypeDesign system
OutputInteractive Figma library
01The problem

Two designers, four versions of the same button.

Without a shared foundation, the product was drifting: spacing decisions were re-litigated every sprint, color palettes diverged, and engineers were inheriting inconsistency as code.

The fix wasn't a Figma library, it was a shared grammar.

02Foundations

A small set of decisions, used everywhere.

01 · Tokens

Color, type, spacing, radius

Named, versioned, mode-aware. One source of truth for both themes.

02 · Primitives

Buttons, inputs, cards, badges

Reusable atoms with documented states. Each one earns its place.

03 · Patterns

Forms, lists, navigation, empty states

Patterns built from primitives, so usage stays consistent without policing.

04 · Docs

Why, when, and what to avoid

Short, scannable rationale per component. Engineers and designers read the same docs.

03Surfaces

Tokens to components, visible at a glance.

Display · 32

Spacing
4pt base·6 stops
Foundations are the boring half. They make the rest fast.
A good system is the one you stop noticing — because everything just feels like it belongs.
04Outcome

Faster decisions, fewer pixel arguments.

The system gave both design and engineering a shared vocabulary. New screens started looking right by default, and reviews stopped revolving around spacing.

  • 100+ components, light + dark, documented and versioned
  • Reduced rework across designers; engineers built against tokens, not pixels
  • Onboarding for new designers dropped from weeks to days

04·UI / UX Case Study

When 10 million learners feel lost, the app is the problem.

A redesign of Babbel, the world's top-selling language app, turning confusion into confidence, one screen at a time.

UX Research UI Design Interaction Design
ProductBabbel · Language learning
RoleUX Research + UI / UX Design
TypeApp redesign · self-initiated
OriginUpLabs challenge · 2022
01The problem

Babbel had the brains. It just didn't have the soul.

Babbel's content was world-class, interactive lessons, speech recognition, podcasts, games. Almost every user improved their grammar and vocabulary, and around 60% even improved their speaking skills.

But the app itself? It was getting in its own way. Users weren't failing at learning, they were failing at using. And in a market where Duolingo makes everything feel like a game, "effective but frustrating" doesn't survive.

10M+

02The deep dive

I didn't just study the app, I became the user.

I used Babbel daily, read hundreds of Play Store reviews, ran competitive analyses against Duolingo, Rosetta Stone and Memrise, and mapped every friction point in the user journey.

  1. 01Personal app usage & observation
  2. 02Play Store review mining, 500+ reviews
  3. 03Competitive analysis, four major competitors
  4. 04Desk research on language-learning outcomes
  5. 05Information architecture mapping
03What was broken

Six fractures in the experience.

Each one small enough to ignore. Together? A death spiral of frustration.

01

Users couldn't find their way. Menus were buried, flows were broken.

04

Grammar improved, but speaking? Users were on their own.

05

Invisible Progress

No word tracking. No milestones. Users couldn't see how far they'd come.

06

Buried Controls

Switching languages meant diving into settings. Every. Single. Time.

04The redesign

Not a facelift. A rethink.

Every screen was rebuilt from first principles: What does the user need right now? What's getting in their way? What would make them come back tomorrow?

Home Screen

The Zeigarnik Hook

A progress bar that whispers "you're almost there." Streak counters that turn learning into a daily ritual. Psychology meets pixels.

↳ Zeigarnik effect, people remember incomplete tasks 90% better
Explore Screen

Content you actually want

Podcasts, articles, games, categorized by what you love, not what we think you should learn. Your interests become your curriculum.

↳ Intrinsic motivation drives 3× more engagement than extrinsic
Connect Screen

The missing piece

Chat with native speakers 1-on-1 or in groups. Because fluency doesn't happen in isolation, it happens in conversation.

↳ Addressing the #1 unmet need from user research
Profile Screen

Your learning DNA

Every saved word, every bookmarked lesson, every milestone, organized beautifully. Your journey, at a glance.

↳ Organized content retrieval reduces cognitive load by 40%
05What I took away

Lessons that shaped how I design.

"UX isn't about features, it's about confidence."

Every interaction should make users feel smarter, not smaller.

"Users don't drop off because they're lazy. They drop off because they're lost."

Navigation isn't just wayfinding, it's the foundation of trust.

"The best UX gets out of your way and helps you feel smart."

Invisible design is the hardest, and most impactful, kind.
